# Omniverse Design Questions v0.1

## Purpose

This document treats the Universal Matrix as a candidate generative architecture and asks what is still required for it to correspond to the measurable universe.

The "creator" framing is used as a design metaphor only. The goal is not to assume the model is physically true, but to identify the missing laws that would have to be specified, derived, or falsified.

The questions are ordered from deepest foundations to emergent real-world observables.

---

## I. Ontology: what actually exists?

### 1. What is the primitive state?

Is the complete local state only

\[
(n,\sigma,\phi,A)
\]

or does a physically complete state also require:

- gauge links,
- conjugate momenta,
- scale index,
- boundary orientation,
- topological sector,
- matter species,
- information/entropy variables?

A real theory needs one explicit state space from which every later observable is derived.

### 2. What is physically real versus bookkeeping?

For each quantity, decide whether it is:

- fundamental,
- emergent,
- gauge redundancy,
- coordinate choice,
- numerical convenience.

In particular:

\[
n,\sigma,\phi,A,\theta,E,F,\ell,k,B_6
\]

cannot all automatically be independent physical observables.

### 3. Is the 108-state core local, global, or repeated?

Does one physical "cell" contain a copy of \(\mathbb Z_{108}\), or is the whole universe one 108-state core?

If repeated, what is the adjacency law between neighboring copies?

If global, how does macroscopic spatial locality emerge?

## II. Space and geometry

### 4. Why exactly three macroscopic spatial dimensions?

The six boundary orientations naturally support

\[
\{\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}.
\]

But the model still needs a reason that these become three large physical dimensions rather than merely three labels.

What mechanism suppresses or internalizes all other state directions?

### 5. What defines physical distance?

A physical metric must determine a measurable interval.

Is distance determined by:

- graph distance,
- number of open links,
- accumulated phase,
- scale-layer ratio,
- field energy,
- a derived metric tensor,
- something else?

Without this, the lattice spacing \(a\) remains arbitrary.

### 6. Is space fundamentally discrete?

If yes, what is the smallest physical separation?

If no, what refinement or renormalization procedure sends the discrete architecture toward a continuum?

### 7. What creates isotropy?

The cubic open adapter picks three axes.

Why should long-distance observations become rotationally invariant?

The model needs either:

- exact microscopic symmetry,
- emergent isotropy,
- randomized orientation averaging,
- a renormalization argument.

### 8. What defines curvature if General Relativity is not primitive?

If gravity is to emerge rather than be inserted, what quantity replaces spacetime curvature?

Candidate possibilities include gradients of:

- phase,
- routing density,
- scale factor,
- gauge energy,
- information density,
- effective refractive structure.

A specific mathematical object is required.

## III. Time and causality

### 9. What is time?

The present Hamiltonian uses an external evolution parameter \(t\).

Is physical time:

- this parameter,
- accumulated phase,
- routing transitions,
- scale transitions,
- entropy increase,
- an emergent clock observable?

### 10. Why does time have one effective direction?

What produces the observed arrow of time?

A complete answer needs a relation between microscopic reversible laws and macroscopic irreversibility.

### 11. What is the causal speed limit?

The weak gauge sector gives

\[
c_{\rm lat}=\sqrt{\beta}
\]

in lattice units.

What fixes \(\beta\), one length unit \(a\), and one time unit \(\tau\) independently so that

\[
c_{\rm phys}=\sqrt{\beta}\frac{a}{\tau}
\]

becomes a prediction rather than a calibration?

### 12. Why is the same causal speed seen by all inertial observers?

A real relativistic correspondence needs an emergent Lorentz symmetry or an experimentally acceptable violation scale.

## IV. Matter

### 13. What is a particle in this architecture?

Is a particle:

- a topological defect,
- a localized gauge excitation,
- a stable polarity configuration,
- a closed routing orbit,
- a soliton,
- a composite of several sectors?

This must be precise enough to simulate.

### 14. What determines particle species?

Why would the architecture yield objects corresponding to:

- electron,
- neutrino,
- quarks,
- photon,
- proton,
- neutron,

rather than arbitrary stable patterns?

### 15. What determines rest mass?

There must be a dimensional observable corresponding to

\[
E_0=mc^2
\]

or an alternative experimentally equivalent relation.

Does mass arise from:

- self-energy,
- trapped circulation,
- topology,
- scale-layer coupling,
- oscillation frequency?

### 16. Why are charge values quantized?

Can electric charge arise from an integer topological invariant or representation label?

The model should explain why charges occur in discrete units rather than arbitrary real numbers.

### 17. What produces spin?

Can half-integer spin emerge from the topology or representation theory of the state space?

A physical model must reproduce the transformation properties of spinors, not merely literal mechanical rotation.

### 18. Why do fermions obey exclusion?

If fermions are emergent excitations, what mathematical property produces antisymmetry or an equivalent exclusion rule?

Does that correspond to electromagnetism, or is it only the first Abelian sector of a deeper gauge structure?

- strong coupling,

### 22. What makes interactions run with energy scale?

Observed couplings change with energy.

Can nested micro-to-macro layers reproduce renormalization-group behavior?

## VI. Gravity without assuming GR

### 23. What observable plays the role of gravitational potential?

If gravity is not primitive, identify one derived quantity whose gradients cause universal attraction for positive inertial energy.

### 24. Why does gravity couple universally?

Electromagnetic attraction/repulsion depends on sign.

Observed gravity is approximately universal for ordinary positive-energy matter.

What mechanism makes the gravitational analogue insensitive to electric polarity?

### 25. Why do inertial and gravitational mass agree?

Any replacement for GR must explain the equivalence principle to experimental accuracy.

### 26. What produces gravitational redshift?

The model must produce a specific predicted ratio between identical clocks at different field states.

### 27. What bends trajectories and light?

A successful model must derive both massive-particle motion and photon propagation from the same underlying structure.

### 28. What reproduces gravitational waves?

If no metric curvature exists fundamentally, what propagating excitation corresponds to observed tensor gravitational-wave polarizations and speed?

### 29. What differs experimentally from GR?

Without at least one observable where

\[
O_{\rm Matrix}\neq O_{\rm GR},
\]

the new theory is not distinguishable from an effective rewriting.

## VII. Quantum behavior

The present phase variables are classical unless a quantum probability structure is specified.

Why should physical predictions use complex amplitudes rather than ordinary probabilities?

### 31. What is the Born rule?

\[

\]

or a demonstrably equivalent observable rule.

### 32. What causes interference?

Can the engine reproduce single-particle interference quantitatively?

### 33. What is entanglement?

A real quantum theory needs composite state spaces capable of Bell-inequality violations.

Classical phase synchronization is insufficient.

### 34. What produces measurement outcomes?

Does the theory contain:

- collapse,
- branching,
- hidden variables,
- decoherence plus an outcome rule,
- something else?

### 35. Can it reproduce Bell/CHSH correlations?

This is a decisive test for any proposed microscopic replacement for standard quantum mechanics.
