# Recursive material-to-Seed correspondence audit

The material recursion and the exact contained Seed/Vesica recursion were developed for different
purposes. They must not be identified merely because both are recursive.

## Two different recursive graphs

The tested material mechanics uses a complete binary tree. Every material edge halves linear
scale:

- exact-level module count: 2^d;
- material scale: (1/2)^d.

The contained universe-port geometry uses every one of the Seed's twelve Vesica interfaces:

- exact-depth address count: 12^d;
- contained child-vessel scale: (1/4)^d.

Therefore one material edge is not one contained Vesica-recursion edge. The branching and scale
laws differ.

Two material levels do recover the same linear scale as one contained Vesica recursion step:

(1/2)^2 = 1/4.

But the topology still does not match: two binary material levels give four descendants, while
one Seed exposes twelve Vesica addresses.

## Alternating scale cadence

There is nevertheless an exact scale correspondence if the intermediate Seed-circle stage is
kept explicit:

- material depth 0: parent vessel, scale 1;
- material depth 1: Seed circle, scale 1/2;
- material depth 2: next contained-vessel scale, 1/4;
- material depth 3: next Seed-circle scale, 1/8.

This is a scale cadence only. It does not turn the material binary tree into the twelve-way
Vesica address tree.

## Mirror-axis binary Seed slice candidate

The Seed ring contains three opposite pairs:

- positions 1 and 4;
- positions 2 and 5;
- positions 3 and 6.

For any one pair, the two selected Seed circles are:

- exactly half the parent vessel radius;
- both contained in the parent vessel;
- exact central mirrors of one another.

Recursing the same construction independently inside each selected circle produces a complete
binary contained circle tree with counts 1, 2, 4, 8 and scales 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 through the
currently tested material depth three.

This makes an opposite-axis Seed slice a geometrically consistent candidate container skeleton
for the binary material hierarchy.

It is not canonical. The Seed has three symmetry-related reciprocal axes, and the present
mathematics does not select one of them. A representative axis can be used for rotation-equivalent
clearance testing, but that must be labeled a candidate gauge/frame choice rather than a unique
physical direction.

## Vesica mirror subsets do not remove the topology ambiguity

The twelve Vesicas form six central-mirror pairs. Selecting two mirror pairs yields a
mirror-closed four-address subset, and there are 15 such subsets.

That count shows why matching the four material grandchildren after two binary levels to four of
the twelve Vesica addresses would require an additional selection rule. No such rule is derived
here.

## Consequence for spatial realization

The next spatial test should not place the binary material modules into all twelve Vesica child
vessels.

Instead, a bounded candidate test may place the replicated 22-body modules inside one
representative opposite-axis Seed-circle binary skeleton. It should then check:

1. module occupied-envelope fit inside each assigned circle;
2. parent-child and sibling body clearance;
3. recurrence of the same normalized clearance under 1/2 scale;
4. central-mirror equivalence of the two child branches;
5. whether any explicit physical joint transform can be assigned between occupied bodies.

The reduced mapped mechanical connector remains a separate energy-consistent coupling until such
a joint transform is constructed. It must not be relabeled as a physical hinge by assumption.

The full twelve-way Vesica recursion remains the complete universe-address geometry and is not
replaced by this material slice candidate.
